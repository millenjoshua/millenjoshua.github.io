Nursing Assessment Guide
Quick reference — vital signs, head-to-toe, risk tools & escalation
Sections
When to assess
Know when a full, routine, or focused assessment is needed.
🚪 On admission / start of shift
Full head-to-toe assessment — do this first, every shift
Full set of vital signs — establish your baseline
All risk assessments — falls, pressure injury, pain
Review handover notes and compare to what you find
Introduce yourself and check patient ID before touching anything
🔄 Routine during the shift
Vital signs as per the observation chart or doctor's orders (e.g. every 4 hours)
Quick visual check every time you enter the room — does the patient look the same?
Pain reassessment after giving analgesia (usually 30–60 min later)
Pressure area check when repositioning (at least every 2 hours for at-risk patients)
🔎 Focused assessment — when triggered
Patient reports a new symptom (pain, SOB, dizziness, nausea)
You notice a change — they look pale, are breathing differently, seem confused
After any procedure, medication, or intervention
After a fall or incident
When a family member tells you something has changed
Rule of thumb: If something feels different — assess immediately. Trust your gut and check vitals.
Vital signs
Normal ranges, warning zones, and when to act.
🌡 Temperature
Normal range36.1 – 37.2 °C normal
Low (hypothermia)< 35.5 °C act now
Mild fever37.3 – 38.4 °C monitor
High fever> 38.5 °C escalate
Think aboutInfection, sepsis, medication reaction, environment
❤️ Heart rate (HR)
Normal60–100 bpm ok
Bradycardia< 50 act
Tachycardia> 120 act
Watch zone50–59 / 101–119 monitor
Also note Rhythm regular or irregular? Strength of pulse?
📊 Blood pressure (BP)
Normal90–140 / 60–90 ok
HypotensionSBP < 90 act
HypertensionSBP > 180 act
Also note Always compare to patient's own baseline, not just textbook normal
🫁 Respiratory rate (RR)
Normal12–20 breaths/min ok
Bradypnoea< 10 act
Tachypnoea> 25 act
Key tip RR is the earliest deterioration sign — count a full 60 seconds
💨 SpO₂ (oxygen sats)
Normal95–100% ok
Concern92–94% monitor
Critical< 92% act now
Note COPD patients may have a lower acceptable baseline (~88–92%) — check orders
😣 Pain score
No pain0 no action
Mild1–3 document, reassess
Moderate4–6 consider analgesia, notify if needed
Severe7–10 act — analgesia + notify team
Use PQRST to describe pain Provokes / Quality / Radiates / Severity / Time (onset & duration)
Head-to-toe assessment
Start every assessment with ABCDE first — it catches life threats before you get to detailed systems.
🛡 ABCDE — do this first
A
Airway
Can the patient speak? Is the airway clear? Listen for stridor, gurgling, or snoring. If they can talk in full sentences → airway is patent.
B
Breathing
Count RR for 60 sec. Check SpO₂. Look for chest rise symmetry, use of accessory muscles, nasal flaring. Listen for wheeze or crackles if trained.
C
Circulation
HR and BP. Skin colour (pink/pale/mottled/cyanosed). Cap refill (press fingertip 5 sec — should return pink in <2 sec). Skin warm or cold/clammy?
D
Disability (neuro)
Use AVPU: Alert / responds to Voice / responds to Pain / Unresponsive. Check orientation — person, place, time. BGL if diabetic or altered consciousness.
E
Exposure
Expose the body (with dignity). Check skin — redness, wounds, bruising, drains, catheters, IV sites. Check temperature. Look for rashes or swelling.
🔍 Systems — work head to toe after ABCDE
Neuro
Neurological
GCS or AVPU. Orientation x3. Pupils equal and reactive to light (PEARL)? Behaviour, mood, speech. Any confusion, agitation, or new drowsiness?
Resp
Respiratory
Breathing effort. Positioning (sitting upright = may be struggling). Cough — productive? Colour of sputum. Oxygen in use — flow rate, device type.
CVS
Cardiovascular
HR and rhythm. BP both arms if indicated. Oedema — ankles, legs (pitting?). JVP elevated? IV access — site clean, patent, no redness?
GI
Gastrointestinal / abdo
Abdomen soft or rigid? Distended? Bowel sounds present? Nausea, vomiting. Last bowel motion. Oral intake — eating and drinking? NG tube if present.
GU
Genitourinary / fluid
Urine output — adequate? (>0.5 mL/kg/hr). Colour and clarity. IDC if present — draining, secure, bag below bladder. Fluid balance chart up to date?
MSK
Musculoskeletal / mobility
Can they move independently? Strength equal bilaterally? Gait safe? Any pain on movement. Post-op patients — limb colour, sensation, and movement distal to surgery.
Skin
Skin integrity
Pressure areas — sacrum, heels, hips, elbows, occiput. Any redness, breakdown, or blistering? Wounds — check dressings. IV sites — no redness, swelling, or leaking.
Psych
Psychosocial
How is the patient feeling? Anxious, distressed, tearful? Support at home? Understanding of their condition? Cultural or communication needs?
Risk tools
Assess on admission and each shift.
🚶 Falls risk — assess on admission + each shift
Previous fall in last 3 months?
On high-risk medications? (sedatives, diuretics, antihypertensives, opioids, hypoglycaemics)
Mobility impaired or uses a walking aid?
Cognitive impairment, confusion, or dementia?
Urinary urgency or incontinence (rushing to toilet)?
Vision problems?
If high risk → bed in lowest position, call bell in reach, non-slip socks, yellow falls alert band, supervise mobilisation.
🩹 Pressure injury risk — Braden scale factors
Sensory perceptionCan they feel and respond to pressure discomfort?
MoistureIs skin exposed to sweat, urine, wound drainage?
ActivityDegree of physical activity — bedbound = highest risk
MobilityCan they change/control body position?
NutritionPoor intake = poor healing and tissue breakdown
Friction / shearBeing pulled up in bed without lifting = shear damage
If at risk → 2-hourly repositioning, pressure-relieving mattress, keep skin dry, document findings.
😣 Pain assessment — use PQRST every time
P — ProvocationWhat makes it better or worse?
Q — QualitySharp, dull, burning, crushing, aching?
R — RadiationDoes it spread? Where to?
S — SeverityScore 0–10. Or use FACES if cognitive impairment.
T — TimeWhen did it start? Constant or intermittent?
Red flags & escalation
If you see any of these — get help immediately. Do not wait.
🚨 Red flags — escalate now
RR < 10 or > 25 breaths/min
SpO₂ < 92% (or below patient's individual target)
HR < 50 or > 120 bpm
SBP < 90 mmHg or > 180 mmHg
Temperature > 38.5 °C or < 35.5 °C
New confusion, agitation, or sudden change in conscious state
AVPU drops from A → V, V → P, or any deterioration
Chest pain (especially crushing, radiating to arm/jaw)
Sudden severe headache or facial droop / arm weakness / slurred speech
Urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/hr for 2+ hours
Uncontrolled bleeding or sudden large wound drainage
Gut feeling — something is just not right
📞 How to escalate — use ISBAR
I
Identify
"Hi, this is [your name], student nurse on Ward 4."
S
Situation
"I'm calling about Molly Clark, DOB 10/01/90. She is deteriorating — RR is now 28 and SpO₂ has dropped to 90%."
B
Background
"She was admitted yesterday for X. Her baseline obs were normal this morning."
A
Assessment
"She appears to be in respiratory distress. She is anxious and using accessory muscles to breathe."
R
Recommendation
"I believe she needs to be reviewed urgently. Can you come now please?"
